Privacy

What Routeloop stores, why, and who else sees it. Written to be read rather than to be defensible.

In effect {{EFFECTIVE}}

The short version

  • Your rides are yours. They are not sold, shared, or used to build a profile of you.
  • There is no advertising here, so there is nothing to feed.
  • Nothing you did not choose is shown to anyone else. Your name, your handle and what appears on a shared ride are all yours to set.
  • You can ask for a copy of everything, or for all of it to be deleted, and I will do it by hand until there is a button.

What is stored

Your account

Your email address, the display name and username you chose, the dates you have held past usernames, and—if you signed in with Google—the account identifier Google issues for you. Sign-in tokens are stored as one-way hashes, so a copy of the database yields no usable logins.

Your profile

Anything you fill in: first and last name, home address and its coordinates, and payment handles for splitting costs on a ride. All of it is optional, all of it is shown only to you, and none of it appears on a shared ride unless you switch it on yourself.

If you sign in with Google, your first and last name are filled in from your Google account as a starting point. They are visible to you on your profile, where you can change or clear them, and to nobody else.

Your rides

The routes, stops, notes and coordinates you enter, plus how many times a shared ride has been opened. If you upload a KML or GPX file, the original is kept so it can be downloaded again.

What you tell us

If you send a bug report, an idea or a question, what is kept is what you wrote, which screen you said it happened on, any screenshot you attached, and your account so we can write back. Bugs and questions are private between you and me. An idea is only ever shown to other riders if I publish it to the board, and nothing is published automatically.

Along with it, the app records a snapshot of what your browser was doing, so you never have to answer a technical question to report something. That snapshot holds: which screen you were on and the version of the app, the site you came from, your browser and operating system, your screen and window size, your language and time zone, whether you had dark mode or reduced motion switched on, how full the app’s local storage was, and any errors or failed requests from the last few minutes.

Two things it deliberately does not hold. It never records where you are: if the app has permission to use your location, what is stored is the word “granted” and never a position. And every web address in it has anything after a question mark stripped off before it is saved, so a link to one of your rides cannot arrive in it by accident.

A screenshot you attach is shrunk by your own phone or computer before it is uploaded, and that re-encoding removes the hidden metadata a photo normally carries—including, on a photo taken with a phone, the GPS coordinates of where it was taken.

You can turn off follow-up messages about a report, and if you do, nothing is sent to you about it.

Technical

Your IP address is used briefly to rate-limit sign-in emails, so the form cannot be used to flood someone’s inbox.

Three cookies exist, and that is the whole list. One keeps you signed in. The other two appear only while you are signing in with Google, protect that handshake against tampering, and expire ten minutes later. All three are strictly functional—the site does not work without them. There is no advertising cookie, no tracking pixel, and no third-party cookie, because there is no advertising here to feed.

Page views are counted with Cloudflare Web Analytics, and have been since July 2026. It is cookieless: it stores nothing on your device, does not follow you from site to site, and builds no profile of you. Visitors in the EU are excluded from it entirely. What it records is that a page was opened—which page, the site you arrived from, the country, and the sort of browser, device and operating system—and nothing that identifies you.

One thing there is worth saying plainly. It records the full address of the page, which means the link to a shared ride appears in my analytics as its own line. It does not record anything after a question mark. Cloudflare already carries every request to this site and sees those addresses regardless; what I cannot do on the plan this runs on is stop them being listed. If that matters for a particular ride, keep it private rather than unlisted.

Who else sees it

Four services are involved in running the app. None of them is sent your rides as a body of data.

  • Google Maps, Places and Routes draw the map, answer your place searches, and work out the roads between two stops. That means Google sees the search terms you type and the coordinates it is asked to route between, in the moment it is asked.
  • Google Sign-In, if you use it, tells Routeloop your email address, whether Google has verified it, and your first and last name. Nothing else is requested.
  • Gmail carries the sign-in email if you use a magic link instead.
  • Cloudflare passes traffic between the internet and the server, and counts page views as described above.

The application and its database run on hardware I own and administer. Your rides are not held by a cloud provider.

What is public

Only what you decide. Every ride starts private. Unlisted means anyone with the link can open it but it is listed nowhere. Public means it can also appear in listings on the site.

A shared ride shows the ride: its routes, stops and notes. It does not show your email, your address, or anything on your profile you have not chosen to share.

Your choices

  • See it—your profile and rides are readable in the app at any time.
  • Change it—everything on your profile is editable, including clearing fields you would rather not store.
  • Take it—ask and I will send you a copy of your account and rides.
  • Delete it—ask and your account and everything attached to it is removed. There is no self-service button yet; until there is, one email does it.

How long things are kept

Your account and rides are kept until you delete them or ask me to. Sign-in links expire in fifteen minutes and are single-use. Rate-limiting records are held for an hour and then discarded.

A report you send, its screenshots and the browser snapshot attached to it are kept for as long as the report is—there is no automatic expiry on them today, and I would rather say so than name a window I am not enforcing. All of it is deleted with your account, which removes it in one step and by design rather than by a cleanup I have to remember to run.

Children

Routeloop is not intended for anyone under 13, and accounts are approved by hand.

Asking

Questions, requests for a copy of your data, or a deletion request all go to the same place: the issue tracker for anything public, or the contact route you signed up through for anything that should not be.
